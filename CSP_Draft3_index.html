Clinical Scholars Program in Academic Hospital Medicine

A one-year advanced training program in adult hospital medicine for early-career physicians building the skills and foundation for a sustainable and fulfilling academic career.

Apply Now

Program Overview

Scholars pair independent clinical practice with coaching, scholarship, and a personalized concentration in POCUS/clinical excellence, medical education, or QI/patient safety. Over one year, they work as attendings across the full range of hospital medicine roles while building the teaching, quality improvement, and scholarly skills that sustain an academic career, supported by Johns Hopkins faculty and a national network of peers.

Personal Path to Academic Excellence

Each Scholar partners with the program co-directors on an Individual Development Plan and chooses a concentration in POCUS/clinical excellence, medical education, or QI/patient safety. Scholars in the POCUS concentration are provided structured support and funding to pursue the SHM POCUS Certificate of Completion.

Clinical Coaching & Mentorship

Scholars receive continuous coaching and mentorship across solo attending, team-based care with advanced practice providers, and consultant / co-management roles in hospital medicine.

Protected Scholarly Time

Scholars spend 70% of an FTE in independent clinical practice, with the remaining 30% protected for the core curriculum, their area of concentration, and scholarly project development.

National Networking

Scholars join peers from the University of Colorado and Weill Cornell Clinical Scholars Programs for a monthly Clinical Reasoning Case Conference. Through real cases, they sharpen advanced Bayesian diagnostic reasoning and build a cross-institutional network of peer scholars.

Curriculum Overview

The core curriculum combines didactics, case-based discussion, direct observation, coaching, and structured reflection across six domains:

Professional Identity Formation & Leadership

Values-based career planning and growth into an academic identity, along with skills in time management, negotiation, and leading multidisciplinary teams.

Bedside Assessment & POCUS

Evidence-based physical exam, hands-on POCUS training with image portfolio support, and fully funded in-person courses with CHEST (Chicago) and Cornell (New York), building toward the SHM POCUS Certificate and/or Johns Hopkins POCUS credentialing.

High-Value Care & Evidence-based Medicine

Advanced Bayesian reasoning, diagnostic stewardship, and value-based care at the bedside.

Consultative & Perioperative Medicine

Didactics on perioperative medicine with real-time 1:1 faculty coaching during clinical weeks on consults.

QI & Patient Safety

Lean Practitioner Certificate training through the Armstrong Institute at the Bloomberg School of Public Health, structured case reviews, patient safety committee participation, and opportunities for more advanced/longitudinal QI work.

Medical Education

Teaching skills training through the Johns Hopkins Institute for Excellence in Education, formal bedside teaching of residents and medical students, and opportunities to pursue scholarship in developing novel curricula.

Compensation

Competitive salary with full benefits, CME fund, Scholars Fund for area of concentration, tuition remission, and access to Johns Hopkins' professional development resources.

Who Should Apply

PGY-3 Internal Medicine residents or early-career hospitalists ready to launch a career in academic hospital medicine.

Eligible candidates must:

  • Hold an MD, DO, or comparable degree from an accredited medical school
  • Complete an ACGME-accredited IM residency before starting the program
  • Be eligible for Maryland medical licensure
  • Demonstrate clinical competency via letters of reference, including from your Program Director
